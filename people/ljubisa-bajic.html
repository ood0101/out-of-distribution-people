Ljubisa Bajic

Two-time AI-chip founder — co-founded Tenstorrent (2016, in his Toronto basement; Grayskull/Wormhole; later a $6.2B company) and then Taalas (Sep 2023), which hardwires an entire LLM into silicon: HC1 is a reticle-limit 815 mm² N6 die with Llama 3.1 8B’s weights cast into a mask-ROM “recall fabric,” independently clocked at ~14K tok/s per user, built by 24 people on $30M of $219M raised. Sold to AMD (definitive agreement Aug 6, 2026, pending). Status: about to become an AMD employee under Vamsi Boppana. The signal is his pattern — he left Tenstorrent Mar 2023 and founded Taalas six months later — so the real inflection is his next exit from AMD. Serbian-origin. NOT Indian. researched: 2026-10-27.

◆ Why tracking, not pursued — the AMD deal reframes the whole team

Taalas is being acquired by AMD. AMD announced a definitive agreement on Aug 6, 2026 (AMD IR release; terms undisclosed; "subject to customary closing conditions and regulatory approvals"; close expected Q4 2026; no completion notice in AMD’s IR feed as of Oct 27 — the definitive check is AMD’s Q3 results on Nov 3). Per EE Times, “Bajic and the Taalas team will join AMD under Vamsi Boppana’s AI organization” — a full acquisition, not an acquihire; Toronto team stays. No retention/earn-out terms are public.

So nobody here is a reach-out target today. They are a post-earn-out departure cohort: a closed 20+-year AMD-Markham → Tenstorrent → Taalas unit that has moved in lockstep at every transition, now re-entering AMD (which most of them left once already). The realistic inflection is a departure ~12–24 months after close (~2028). Indexed so the fund is pre-researched and first-call when it happens.

◆ The Alpha — measured honestly

He is the only founder in the AI-chip cohort who built and scaled one unicorn (Tenstorrent) before starting a second company — and the second one shipped a reticle-limit die with 24 people and $30M. Architect of Grayskull (620 mm², 120 Tensix cores) and Wormhole (670 mm², 100G scale-out) at Tenstorrent; before that ~10 years at ATI/AMD (GPU power efficiency — Keller: “Raja Koduri actually told him later on that a lot of the power/performance improvement they got came from the work he did”), a year as Senior Architect at NVIDIA, then back to AMD as Director of IC Design working with Keller on the Zen-era reset. HC1’s genuine step is density: a 4-bit weight stored and multiplied on one transistor, mask-ROM at DRAM-level density, so a whole 8B model sits on one die with zero weight movement — no HBM, no advanced packaging, air-cooled at ~200 W, re-spun for a new model by changing two metal masks (~2-month TSMC turnaround). An Aug 2026 arXiv paper on compute-in-ROM cites HC1 as the sole commercial precedent. 14 patent families filed.

Calibrated honestly: the speed is real (EE Times 15K+, Karl Freund 14,357, a July black-box probe ~14.2K sustained) but the marketing outran it — “73× vs H200” is a batch-1 latency number against a 3-/6-bit quality-degraded model (one H200 emits more aggregate tokens than HC1 gives one user; concurrent users/chip never disclosed, so $/token claims are unverifiable). Zero named customers Feb→Aug; the spring/summer roadmap (second model, 20B/chip) shows no public evidence of shipping; a pre-revenue sale six months post-launch with ~$170M still in the bank is not what working standalone economics look like. What AMD bought is the team and the 2-month model-to-silicon flow — model-specific silicon as a decode-stage companion to Instinct GPUs, a feature inside a GPU roadmap, not a platform business. Back the operator next time; the product was a clever niche.

Voice — and the outreach insight almost nobody will have

  • Launch essay (Feb 2026): “AI inference is the most critical computational workload that humanity has ever faced, and the one that stands to gain the most from specialization… the production of optimal silicon for each individual model.” · “a system that does not depend on difficult or exotic technologies, no HBM, advanced packaging, 3D stacking, liquid cooling, high speed IO.” · “Taalas is a precision strike, in a world where deep-tech startups approach their chosen problems like medieval armies besieging a walled city.”
  • EE Times (Feb 2026): “Essentially, we looked for ways to make painful tradeoffs in flexibility for the sake of economics and speed.” · “Because we’re hardwiring it, the margin for error is basically zero.” · on a 671B model needing ~30 chips: “30 incremental tape-outs, which is the annoying part, but the tape-outs are pretty cheap.” · “The big thing at the root of this idea is the assumption that the customer is willing to commit to this.”
  • Next Platform: “we can put a weight and do the multiply associated with it all in one transistor… this is not nuclear physics – it is fully digital… basically our whole effort ended up being a throwback to the 1970s.” Candor in the essay: “Our debut model is clearly not on the leading edge.”
  • The insight: Taalas is a deliberate inversion of his own 2021 thesis. At Tenstorrent he argued flexibility was the edge — “The one thing that Grayskull can do, which… most of the alternatives cannot do, is conditional computation… Adding programmability isn’t free. We think we strike a good balance.” He then walked the entire flexibility→total-specialization arc himself. Open any future conversation there.
