◆ The Alpha — what’s actually special, measured against the field

The alpha is NOT the ultrasound physics (functional ultrasound / fUS and microbubble super-resolution localization are established academic techniques — Mickael Tanter / Charlie Demené at Physics for Medicine Paris; the 2015 ULM Nature paper). The alpha is the combination of three things almost nobody else is doing at once:

(1) A cost/scale inversion as a deliberate wedge. The incumbent BCI world is invasive electrodes (Neuralink, Precision Neuroscience, Paradromics) or room-scale machines (MRI, MEG). Aleph’s bet is that a commodity ultrasound-on-chip (Butterfly Network’s Embedded™ program — a handheld probe that costs ~$2K, not $1M) is the substrate that makes neural imaging cheap and scalable enough to put on everyone. They claim "MRI-level detail… without drilling or the bulk of an MRI machine" and a 3D vascular volume "100× greater volumetrically than comparable CT." Whether or not that headline survives scrutiny, the strategic framing — ride a Moore’s-law-y consumer chip into neuro — is the venture-shaped insight.

(2) The AI/ML second act — stated, not yet built (this is the on-thesis crux). Their own blog is explicit that today’s result is signal processing, and that the real play is learned: “A standard ultrasound probe receives terabytes of data per hour, but the typical processing pipeline compresses this down to just 0.1% of the original. It’s built on hand-engineered features… We believe end-to-end machine learning, trained on large enough datasets, will recover far more signal.” They are “building the world’s largest dataset of neurovascular ultrasound” and explicitly cite MindEye (fMRI→image reconstruction) as the north star — “communicate in latents—the raw thoughts that words attempt to capture.” That is a data-moat + neural-decoding foundation-model thesis wearing a hardware demo as its cold-start. The careers page lists Machine Learning Engineer first of 10 roles.

(3) “Achievement despite no scaffolding” in its purest form. His verbatim tell: “Doing science outside of the context of a lab turned out to be easier than we thought - it’s been so fun!” — a 5-person team with no institution, no PhDs required, self-experimenting (he got “an improvised last-minute fade haircut for better signal quality… to see inside your brain”), open-sourcing the data and code, and producing a result that got a national-medtech-press launch in ~4 months (domain registered 8 Feb 2026 → public 25 Jun 2026). This is the owner’s exact taste: rate-of-change + no permission.

Calibrated honestly (where the alpha is thin): The current, verifiable artifact is a hardware/imaging demo, and the science it’s built on (fUS, ULM) is not theirs. The AI/ML core that would make this on-thesis for this fund is aspirational — stated in a blog post, staffed by a job opening, not yet shipped. Lev personally is not the neuro-imaging domain expert on the team (that’s co-founder Raffi Hotter — ex-Adam Cohen lab @ Harvard, ex-Google X neurotech); Lev’s spike is physics + AI-agents/software (Clippinator). His research record is thin and young (1 paper, not first author, on metamaterials — unrelated to neuro). So: real venture, real OOD founder energy, genuine AI/ML intent — but read this as a frontier-neurotech company with an AI/ML wedge, not (yet) an AI/ML-foundation-model company. The tier call below turns entirely on how you weight that.
