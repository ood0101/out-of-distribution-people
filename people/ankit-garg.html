◆ The Alpha — what makes him worth indexing, measured honestly

Operator scaling (Garg–Gurvits–Oliveira–Wigderson, FOCS 2016) is a program-launching result. It gave the first deterministic polynomial-time algorithm for a natural problem — non-commutative rational identity testing / the word problem for the free skew field / singularity of symbolic matrices in non-commuting variables — that previously had only exponential-time algorithms. Unconditional derandomization of a natural algebraic problem is rare and prized. It bridged PIT, invariant theory (null-cone, degree bounds), operator/quantum theory (completely-positive maps, Sinkhorn-type scaling), and geodesic-convex optimization — and launched a line: algorithmic Brascamp–Lieb (STOC’17), geodesic operator scaling (STOC’18), non-commutative optimization / moment polytopes (FOCS’19), tensor scaling / quantum marginals (FOCS’18), with Avi Wigderson (Turing’23 / Abel’21).

ML-foundations reach (more than an ivory-tower theorist): his top-cited work is distributed statistical-estimation lower bounds (Braverman–Garg–Ma–Woodruff, STOC’16, 235 cites — communication-constrained / federated estimation limits); operator scaling is the non-commutative generalization of matrix scaling = Sinkhorn = entropic OT; and he has learning-theory-adjacent work (learning arithmetic formulas / tensor decomposition, with Neeraj Kayal & Chandan Saha).

Calibrated honestly: operator scaling is a deep-subfield landmark, not a broadly-famous or deployed mega-result — and no major individual prize (Gödel/Fulkerson) is confirmed for the line. He is an upper-tier mid-career algebraic-complexity theorist with one field-shaping result and a strong follow-on program — not a singular field-dominating figure. His frontier-ML output is either a decade old (distributed estimation) or very recent and thin (one retrieval paper). A theorist migrating toward applied ML, not yet a frontier-ML producer. h-22 / 1,884 cites (healthy spread — 7 papers >100 — not one lucky hit; modest absolutes because theory is a low-citation field).
